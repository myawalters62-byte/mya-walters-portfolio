My Journey

I'm a business professional who chose problems over job titles and learned that career clarity comes from honest audit.

I started in political science at Sydney University—drawn to ideas and power—then left when COVID hollowed out campus. That brave call taught me that environments matter. I moved to fashion, where I interned at Carla Zampatti and heard a founder's story that crystallized a question my entrepreneur parents had planted: What does it look like to build something that makes lives better?

From fashion, I entered enterprise software sales at SafetyCulture, a unicorn tech company. For three years, I've managed complex deals, progressed from BDR to Enterprise Account Executive in 33 months, and hosted industry conferences. I've learned that people don't buy products—they buy change they can feel. That insight bridges my past.

Right now, I'm finishing my Bachelor of Business at RMIT while carrying quota. The degree gave frameworks to instincts: complex problem-solving sharpened deal strategy, global mindset formalized what political science began, and leadership through group work stress-tested how I run teams.

My next five years split into two divergent paths: climbing to commercial leadership in enterprise SaaS, or building circular-fashion technology where supply-chain visibility meets AI. Both run through the same engine: entrepreneurship as what I want to do, and communication as how anything gets done. Either path, I'm accumulating the same capital—networks, operating experience, and the story that connects them.

Deal Breakdown & Narrative ▼

Deal Overview: This case study represents a complex enterprise sales cycle with a leading QHSE (Quality, Health, Safety, Environment) organization. The client was managing multiple operational challenges across their field teams, including manual reporting processes, compliance gaps, and limited real-time visibility into frontline operations.

Challenge: Rather than treating this as a "software replacement" opportunity, I identified that the real problem was operational—the client needed a frontline-first approach to data collection and visibility, not just another system to manage. This required reframing SafetyCulture's positioning from IT/compliance-focused to operations-focused.

Solution Architecture: I designed a phased proof of concept that demonstrated ROI in 90 days. The approach focused on: (1) frontline-first mobile capture, (2) real-time dashboarding for site managers, (3) automated compliance reporting, (4) integration with existing systems. This architecture separated SafetyCulture's value from competing solutions and positioned it as foundational infrastructure, not an add-on.

Commercial Strategy: The deal involved multiple stakeholders—operations, IT, finance, and compliance. I mapped each stakeholder's success metrics and designed a licensing model that showed ROI against each metric. Rather than high-touch enterprise pricing, I modeled consumption-based licensing around actual field team usage, which reduced perceived risk and accelerated approval.

Outcome: Successfully closed a complex enterprise deal. The cycle demonstrated consultative sales mastery: listening more than pitching, positioning around client problems (not product features), designing proof of value, and navigating complex stakeholder objections with commercial creativity.
